## 1. `/VSET1` shorts to the ground pour — Error

Two F.Cu segments on `/VSET1` sit at 0.0000 mm from `ZoneB_GND_F`, against a
0.1270 mm rule:

```
@(72.1000, 97.9243)  Track [/VSET1] on F.Cu, length 0.2465 mm
@(72.1000, 100.0025) Track [/VSET1] on F.Cu, length 2.0782 mm
```

`/VSET1` is `R21.1 + U2.17`, and R21's other end is already on GND.

**Root cause — corrected, and it was not a routing error.** The offending
copper is a **ground stitching via**, `(71.50, 100.00)` in the `STITCH` list in
`tools_route.py`. R21 is rotated −90°, which puts its VSET1 pad at:

```
R21 pad 1 (VSET1)   x span 71.7800 .. 72.4200
stitch via 0.60 mm  x span 71.2000 .. 71.8000
                              overlap 0.02 mm
```

A 0.02 mm overlap between a GND via and a VSET1 pad. KiCad merged them, which
is why DRC reported it as a VSET1 *track* at zero clearance to the pour — the
symptom pointed at the routing, the cause was in the stitching array.

**Fixed** — the via moved to `(70.90, 100.00)`, which holds 0.58 mm to R21's
pad and 1.09 mm to C25's. R21 and the `/VSET1` net are unchanged; nothing
needed deleting, so finding 3 below is a documentation question, not a fix.

This is worth remembering as a class: a stitching array is generated from a
coordinate list that knows nothing about what is placed, so moving or rotating
any part can put a pad under a via, and the violation surfaces against the
*net that got merged*, not against the via.

## 2. Sense electrodes are isolated copper — Error

Four `isolated_copper` warnings:

```
@(69.0, 159.0)  Zone 'SENSE1_electrode'    [/SENSE1] F.Cu
@(69.0, 122.0)  Zone 'SENSE2_electrode'    [/SENSE2] F.Cu
@(67.3, 112.0)  Zone 'ZoneC_GUARD'         [/SHLD]   B.Cu, In2.Cu  (x2)
@(67.0, 105.8)  Zone 'SenseEscape_GUARD'   [/SHLD]   B.Cu, In2.Cu  (x2)
```

The zones carry the correct nets — `SENSE1_electrode`→`/SENSE1`,
`SENSE2_electrode`→`/SENSE2`, both guard zones→`/SHLD`. What is missing is
copper joining each zone to U3's pins: `/SENSE1` (U3.2 CIN1), `/SENSE2`
(U3.3 CIN2) and `/SHLD` (U3.1 SHLD1, U3.6 SHLD2) have no track anywhere on
the board. These three nets are the entire measurement function.

## 3. BUCK1 unused but VSET1 populated — Design question

|---|---|---|

| 3 | SW1 | no-connect |

For comparison, Nordic's reference layout (`nRF54L15_qfaa_4L_config1_pcb.pdf`)
places C2, C12 and C5 directly against the DECA/DECRF pins, and C3/C10/L1/FB1

C1 at 5.18 mm from DECD matters more than its position in the list suggests:
DECD is the DC/DC output node and carries the switching ripple.

C27 at 9.58 mm from the SHT45's VDD is a separate case — U4 sits on the
jut-out at board (39.8, 20.6) while C27 is at (31.5, 20.6), so the bypass is
on the far side of the enclosure wall from the part it decouples.

**Attempted and reverted.** The distance is structural, not an oversight.
Three rule areas govern the tab: `SHT45_Jut` bans pour and vias across all of
it, `NoCopperSHT45` bans tracks as well under the die. So both rails must come
around U4 from the east, and exactly one strip could carry a bridging cap:

| | width |
|---|---|
| east strip, x 101.045–102.000 | 0.955 mm |
| 0402 rotated | 1.010 mm — does not fit |
| 0201 rotated | 0.790 mm — fits |

A 0201 was swapped in and placed at (101.47, 60.60) rot 90, inside a 0.06 mm
window bounded by U4's courtyard on one side and the 0.300 mm board-edge
clearance on the other. **The placement is legal; the routing is not.** The two
hops onto its pads kept merging with the long tab runs and dragging `+3V3` back
across U4's pads and into the die keepout — 10 DRC violations including a
`+3V3`/`/SCL` short. Reverted to the known-good state.

It is also the weakest electrical case on the board. The SHT45 is an I²C part
at 400 kHz drawing microamps idle and ~60 mA only while its heater fires, on
millisecond timescales; ~10 nH of loop is immaterial there. Contrast C9, where
5 nH flipped a 2.4 GHz shunt inductive.

To close it later: place the 0201 at (101.47, 60.60) rot 90 and draw the two
0.3 mm hops **by hand**, keeping both east of x = 101.2 so neither crosses
`NoCopperSHT45`.

**Root cause worth noting:** every support passive is a 0402 HandSolder
footprint where Nordic's reference uses 0201. The larger pads and the
elongated hand-solder geometry are part of why the caps cannot sit as close.
That is an assembly choice, not an error, but it caps how much of this gap is
recoverable.

## 4a. C9's shunt branch was inductive — **FIXED**

Not in the original audit; found by re-reading Nordic's reference against our
stackup. Nordic's rule 2 is that C9's ground must reach *only* the bottom
ground layer, isolated from the rest. The isolation was implemented correctly —
the via is on `/RF_C9_RETURN_BOTTOM`, so `ZoneB_GND` on In1.Cu clears it — but **there was
no bottom ground layer to land on.**

Nordic's config-1 4-layer has ground on L2, L3 **and** L4 (visible on pages 3–5
of `nRF54L15_qfaa_4L_config1_pcb.pdf`: two solid inner ground planes and a
poured bottom). Ours has ground on In1.Cu only — In2.Cu is the 3V3 plane and
B.Cu in Zone B was bare. So the return ran ~5 mm as a narrow B.Cu track to the
nearest stitching via, and a narrow track is an inductor:

| leg | mm | nH |
|---|---|---|
| F.Cu stub to the via | 0.83 | 0.50 |
| through via F.Cu→B.Cu | 1.49 | 1.34 |
| B.Cu via → NT2 | 1.30 | 0.52 |
| B.Cu NT2 → stitch via | 4.20 | 1.68 |
| via B.Cu → In1.Cu | 1.27 | 1.15 |
| **total** | | **5.18** |

At 2.4 GHz that is **+j78.2 Ω** against C9's own −j33.2 Ω, so the shunt branch
came out at **+45 Ω — inductive**. A 2.0 pF shunt capacitor behaving as an
inductor. Exactly the failure the C6 stub had, one layer down.

**Fixed** by adding `ZoneB_GND_B`, a B.Cu ground pour local to the RF return
(x 78.0–88.0, y 51.5–62.0), so the via lands in copper that spreads instead of
a track that doesn't. Path is now stub + via + a short hop: **2.04 nH,
+j30.7 Ω, branch −2.5 Ω and still capacitive.** The 4.20 mm detour is deleted.

The pour starts 1.20 mm east of the `/ANT` column at x = 76.80, so it never
sits under the RF trace and the 50 Ω microstrip-over-In1.Cu geometry from
LAYOUT.md §2 is untouched. Three existing stitching vias — (81.50, 55.00),
(84.00, 55.00), (86.50, 55.00) — tie it to In1.Cu, all ≥ 4.70 mm from the trace.

**Worth checking on the VNA at bring-up** along with the rest of the matching
network; the inductance figures are estimates at 0.4–0.9 nH/mm, not extractions.

## 6. BUCK2 switching loop — Layout

Measured from pad geometry, board coordinates:

```
U2.5  SW2    ( 7.074, 56.250)
L10.1        ( 3.275, 53.500)   <- SW2 side, FAR pad
L10.2        ( 4.725, 53.500)   <- VOUT2 side, near pad
C24.1 +3V3   ( 3.138, 57.000)
C24.2 PVSS2  ( 4.862, 57.000)
U2.6  PVSS2  ( 7.074, 56.750)

enclosed area  : 10.03 mm²  (convex hull)
loop perimeter : 14.43 mm
```

> **Correction.** The first version of this finding gave the enclosed area as
> 5.50 mm². That figure was wrong. As placed, the pad sequence self-intersects
> — the path runs x = 3.275 → 4.725 → 3.138, doubling back on itself — and the
> shoelace formula cancels signed area across a bowtie, understating it. The
> real enclosed area is 10.03 mm², and it is the same before and after the
> rotation below, because the four corners of the loop do not move.
> `tools_loop_area.py` now refuses to report a shoelace figure for a
> self-intersecting path.

**L10 is oriented backwards.** `/SW2` connects U2.5 to L10.1, which is the pad
*further* from U2 — so the highest-dv/dt net on the board runs the length of
the inductor body to reach its own pad. Rotating L10 180° shortens both halves:

| | As placed | Rotated 180° |
|---|---|---|
| SW2 run (U2.5 → L10 pad 1) | 4.69 mm | **3.62 mm** |
| VOUT run (L10 pad 2 → C24.1) | 3.84 mm | **3.50 mm** |
| Enclosed area | 10.03 mm² | 10.03 mm² |

There is no trade — both runs improve — but note what it does and does not buy:
it takes 1.07 mm off the switching node, and it does **not** reduce the loop
area. Shrinking the loop needs L10 and C24 moved closer to U2, which is a
placement change, not an orientation one.

`/SW2` has no copper at all, so this costs nothing to do now.

## 8. Track angles — Geometry — **FIXED**

> `tools_lint_angles.py` passes, and `tools_route.py` can no longer emit an

## Recommended order for Phase 3

Findings 1, 2, 3 and 6 all sit in the power/PMIC and sense blocks and all
touch nets that currently have no copper. Doing power/PMIC first — as the
design already specifies — lets the L10 rotation, the VSET1 decision and the
BUCK2 loop tightening land before anything is routed around them.
